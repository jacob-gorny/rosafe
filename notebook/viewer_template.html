Real outcomes are shown only for the roads that average 8 or more crashes a day, and only as a level, never a count, so no one can work out a day's crashes from this page (TxDOT's terms).

How the score is made

A gradient-boosting model reads the weather that day and over the weeks before it, plus how each road is built. It predicts each road's crashes and divides by the road's normal for that weekday. It never sees the crashes of the day it scores.

Limits

Scores are per day, not per hour. They use the weather that was measured that day; a live app would use a forecast (in our tests, with only the day before's weather the simpler model's edge almost vanished, so a live version needs a good forecast of today's rain). In 2023 most of the edge came from whether it rained; in 2024 the weather beyond rain added a real gain too. City streets and toll roads have no surface, lanes or traffic data, so they're scored from road class, speed limit and their own crash history. The app alerts only on days with rain or ice; on dry days the model's flags were much weaker, so they're left out. Map lines are averages of at least 5 crash positions, so they're approximate.

Sources

Crash data: Texas Department of Transportation, Crash Records Information System (CRIS), 2020 to 2024, through TxDOT's public map layer. This page holds model scores and averaged road shapes, not crash records. Weather: NOAA GHCN-Daily, DFW Airport station (USW00003927).
